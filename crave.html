Crave

I interviewed three Crave viewers about finding content, recommendations and playback issues.

I developed six design principles covering Home, content discovery, profiles, recommendation controls and playback reliability. Two desktop wireframes explored Home and documentary browsing.

2025 study

User interviews

I began with ten assumptions and focused the interviews on discovery, personalization and technical friction. I used a 15-question guide, transcribed three interviews and grouped the accounts into needs, comments, suggestions and emotions.

The content itself still mattered: all three described their usage changing with the shows or series they were watching.

An assumption I reconsidered

I expected people to arrive with a title in mind. One interviewee usually did. The other two usually browsed without one.

Phase 2 strategy

Design recommendations

I developed six design principles from the study. Some became features in the wireframes, while others remained proposals for further design or technical investigation.

Simplify Home

Phase 2 / p. 9

Research basis: Participants described different starting intentions and problems with repetitive or confusing categories.

Recommendation: Use fewer, clearer sections, remove repeated content placements, improve the hierarchy and keep Continue Watching prominent.

Home wireframe

Continue Watching and By Genre are visible in the original Home layout. A reduction in overload was not measured.

Use more specific categories

Phase 2 / p. 10

Research basis: Participants wanted clearer genre choices; one requested specific documentary subjects.

Recommendation: Add subgenres and useful filters, avoid duplicate listings, offer an editorial “Hot this month” category and let viewers pin preferred categories.

Wireframe and selected prototype states

Documentary subjects appear in the wireframe. The later prototype develops subject and length selection. Editorial and pinning features remain proposals.

Make profile setup more visible

Phase 2 / p. 11

Research basis: Two interviewees were unsure about, or did not recognize, individual profile options.

Recommendation: Prompt profile creation during onboarding, make Add Profile easier to find, collect favourite genres or shows, and keep viewing histories separate.

Home wireframe cues

The layout includes a profile control, signed-in context and a setup reminder. Guided setup and preference checks were not developed into complete flows.

Offer more ways to browse

Phase 2 / p. 12

Research basis: Two interviewees usually browsed without a particular title in mind.

Recommendation: Explore curated collections, editorial playlists, a shuffle or “Surprise Me” option, mood filters and custom saved lists.

Genre route shown; other ideas proposed

The Home wireframe provides a By Genre route. Collections, shuffle, mood filters and custom lists remained strategy proposals, not tested features.

Improve playback reliability

Phase 2 / p. 13

Research basis: Accounts included lost viewing positions, refreshing after time away and occasional TV friction.

Recommendation: Preserve playback position, improve resuming after inactivity, review buffering and device consistency, and add a Still Watching check.

Technical investigation proposed

Playback was outside the wireframes and browsing prototype. The interviews did not establish device benchmarks or comparative performance.

Explain recommendations and add controls

Phase 2 / p. 14

Research basis: Recommendation relevance was mixed, and the viewing context or recommendation basis was not always clear.

Recommendation: Explain why content is suggested, allow preference overrides or resets, offer Don’t Recommend This, adjust category visibility and clarify streaming-quality information.

Feedback cue shown; settings proposed

The Home wireframe includes Not For Me. Recommendation explanations, reset settings and quality indicators remained proposals.

Source: Phase 2, “Design Principles and Recommendations,” pp. 9–14. The recommendations include design proposals as well as responses directly supported by interview accounts. Their effectiveness was not tested.

Project status

Outcome and next steps

The coursework produced a research-based strategy and two wireframes. The later work develops documentary browsing and translates the Home layout into high-fidelity visual studies. Profile setup, feedback behaviour and playback recovery still need separate work.

2025 coursework
I completed the three interviews, synthesis, strategy, six design principles, journey and task models, and two desktop wireframes independently.
2026 refinement
A separate Figma documentary-browsing prototype. Functional checks covered its links, subject and length persistence, clearing, menu dismissal and scrolling. The additional November Home mockups are visual studies; their profile and feedback flows have not been implemented or tested.
What I would test next
Give viewers a documentary subject, then check whether they can interpret the labels, find a relevant option and understand the active refinement. Profile awareness and playback recovery need separate evaluation.

No post-design usability study, recommendation-system test or measured retention outcome is available. The prototype covers desktop browsing; playback, title details, complete search/profile flows and mobile behaviour remain outside its scope.
